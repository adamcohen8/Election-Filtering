2026 Senate Elec(cid:415)on
Democrat Troy Jackson has opened a lead over incumbent Republican Senator Susan Collins in the home stretch of the race for U.S.
Senate in Maine. Including those leaning towards a candidate, 54% of likely voters say they will or already have voted for Jackson, 45%
say they will vote or already have voted for Collins, less than 1% support another candidate, and less than 1% are undecided. In
September, 51% supported Jackson while 47% supported Collins. Nearly all Democrats (98%) support Jackson, up from 94% in
September, while 98% of Republicans support Collins, unchanged since September (98%). Just over half of Independents (53%) support
Jackson, up from 49% in September, while 45% support Collins.
Jackson has become the more personally popular candidate: 46% of likely voters have a favorable opinion of Jackson, 46% have an
unfavorable opinion, and 8% are neutral, yielding a net favorability ra(cid:415)ng (the percentage who have a favorable opinion minus the
percentage who have an unfavorable opinion) among likely voters of -1, up from -8 in September. Thirty-nine percent have a favorable
opinion of Collins, 54% have an unfavorable opinion, and 7% are neutral, yielding a net favorability ra(cid:415)ng of -15, largely unchanged since
September (-14).

Pine Tree State Poll Methodology
These findings are based on the latest Pine Tree State Poll, conducted by the University of New Hampshire Survey Center. Pine Tree
State Panel members were sent an invita(cid:415)on to complete the survey and 1,262 completed the survey online, yielding a response rate of
46%. In addi(cid:415)on, 460 Maine residents completed the survey a(cid:332)er receiving an invita(cid:415)on via text message to complete the survey.
Overall, 1,722 Maine residents completed the survey online between October 27 and October 31, 2026. The margin of sampling error
for the survey is +/- 2.4 percent. Included in the sample were 1,654 likely general elec(cid:415)on voters (margin of sampling error +/- 2.4
percent), 843 of whom reside in Maine's First Congressional District (MOE +/- 3.4 percent) and 802 of whom reside in Maine's Second
Congressional District (MOE +/- 3.5 percent). In addi(cid:415)on to poten(cid:415)al sampling error, all surveys have other poten(cid:415)al sources of
non-sampling error including ques(cid:415)on order effects, ques(cid:415)on wording effects, and non-response. These MSE's have not been adjusted
for design effect. The design effect for the survey is 2.3%.
The Pine Tree State Panel is a probability-based web panel of Maine residents. Approximately 3,200 Maine adults have been recruited
from phone, text-to-web, or mail-to-web surveys sent to randomly-selected phone numbers or addresses. Panelists are then asked to
par(cid:415)cipate in online surveys sponsored by the UNH Survey Center.
Data were weighted by respondent sex, age, educa(cid:415)on, and region of the state to targets from the most recent American Community
Survey (ACS) conducted by the U.S. Census Bureau, as well as party registra(cid:415)on levels provided by the ME Secretary of State and to
2024 elec(cid:415)on results in Maine. Surveys were taken in English, all respondent data included passes Qualtrics data quality checks, and
single-use survey links were used to prevent mul(cid:415)ple submissions by respondents.
The number of respondents in each demographic below may not equal the number reported in cross-tabula(cid:415)on tables as some
respondents choose not to answer some ques(cid:415)ons. Due to rounding, percentages may not sum to 100%. All coding of open-ended
responses is conducted using AI coding so(cid:332)ware. For more informa(cid:415)on about the methodology used in this Pine Tree State Poll, contact
Dr. Andrew Smith at (603) 862-2226 or by email at andrew.smith@unh.edu.
