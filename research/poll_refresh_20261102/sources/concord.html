To:Interested Parties
From:Concord Public Opinion Partners
Re:South Dakota Statewide Survey
Date:October 8, 2026

Key Findings

Independent Brian Bengs holds a +1-point lead over Republican Mike Rounds in the Senate race (43% Bengs / 42% Rounds / 10% undecided): Polling conducted by Concord Public Opinion Partners of likely 2026 General Election voters in South Dakota shows Senator Rounds trailing his party's candidates for governor and the House. Rounds leads by +67 points among voters with a favorable view of President Trump (77% / 10%), while Bengs leads by +58 points among voters with an unfavorable view of Trump (71% / 13%).
Rounds' deficit is driven by Republican drop-off: Rounds leads among Republicans by +36 points (61% / 25%), while in the race for Governor Republican Larry Rhoden leads by +47 points (65% / 18%); one in four Republicans backs Bengs, and 35% of Republicans view Rounds unfavorably. Bengs leads Democrats by +58 points (68% / 10%), and only 16% of Democrats have an unfavorable view of him.
Republican incumbent Larry Rhoden holds a +10-point lead over Democrat Dan Ahlers for governor (48% Rhoden / 38% Ahlers / 10% undecided): Four percent choose someone else. Rhoden runs well ahead of the rest of the Republican ticket: his +10-point lead is +11 points better than Rounds' -1-point deficit in the Senate race (42% Rounds / 43% Bengs) and +7 points better than Jackley's +3-point lead for the House (44% / 41%). He also runs ahead of President Trump: his 48% vote share is +4 points above Trump's 44% favorable rating, and his +10-point lead is +20 points better than Trump's -10-point net favorability (44% / 54%).
Rounds and President Trump both have negative net favorability ratings (Rounds -4 points, 44% / 48%; Trump -10 points, 44% / 54%): Trump's South Dakota rating is +9 points better than his current national average. Both challengers have positive net favorability ratings: Bengs has a +14-point positive net favorability rating (38% favorable / 24% unfavorable) and Ahlers a +15-point positive rating (37% / 22%); 37% and 41% respectively have no opinion or are neutral.

New Jersey Governor Statewide Findings

To:Interested Parties
From:Concord Public Opinion Partners
Re:New Jersey Statewide Survey Results
Date:October 24, 2025

Key Findings

Polling conducted by Concord Public Opinion Partners of likely 2025 General Election voters in New Jersey shows Mikie Sherrill leading Jack Ciattarelli in the race for Governor by 9 points (49% to 40%). The poll found Sherrill leading with key subgroups, including a 9-point advantage with Independents (48% to 39%), 63-points with Black voters (70% to 7%) and 15-points with Latino voters (50% to 35%). Sherrill maintains a strong lead amongst women +15 over Ciattarelli (53% to 38%), and a narrow lead with men +3 at (45% to 42%).
Sherrill is viewed positively by a plurality of voters (45% favorable / 42% unfavorable) but Ciattarelli is under water (41% favorable / 49% unfavorable): Sherrill enjoys a +3 favorability rating while Ciattarelli is viewed more negatively (-8), giving him the same net negative rating as President Trump (44% favorable / 52% unfavorable).
New Jersey voters overwhelmingly support K-12 education policy proposals designed to improve 3rd grade literacy, including accountability measures, expanded school choice options, and implementation of innovative approaches to accelerate student learning and growth: Over 8 in 10 voters (82%) expressed support for accountability measures that would measure student performance, provide early interventions to struggling readers and hold schools accountable for results. The proposal received strong support across race, gender, and party, including 92% from Democrats, 71% from Republicans, 91% from Black voters, and 92% backing from Latinos.
Close to two-thirds (63%) of the voters said they support policies to expand school choice options, including interdistrict enrollment, additional public charter school seats, and subsidies to parents to pay for transportation costs and out-of-school enrichment activities. Democrats (62%) and Republicans (62%) were equally supportive but Black voters (72%) expressed the most support.
A strong majority of voters (57%) showed support for innovative approaches, including the exploration of Artificial Intelligence to accelerate learning and growth, supporting innovative models such as microschools, and scaling what works best. Strongest support came from Democrats (67%), Black voters (68%), and voters between the ages of 35-49 (63%).
Almost two-thirds of New Jersey voters want to see the next governor opt-in to the federal tax scholarship school choice program known as the Educational Choice for Children Act (ECCA): Close to two-thirds of voters (65%) said they support the next governor opting-in to ECCA while only 21% opposed. ECCA is most popular amongst voters of color, with 79% of Black voters strongly or somewhat supporting, as well as 76% of Latino voters and 72% of Asian voters. Democratic voters were the most supportive, with 71% in favor of opting in, compared to 62% of Republicans and 61% of Independents.

Methodology

This survey was conducted between 10/16-18 with a sample size of 605 respondents. Data was collected by contacting likely voters for the November 2025 NJ Governor election via live calls to cell phones and landlines and Text-to-Web surveys. The margin of error for this survey is +/- 3.92%. Data was weighted by the following: Race, Age, Gender, Party, and College Attainment.

New Mexico Poll Findings

To:Interested Parties
From:Concord Public Opinion Partners
Re:New Mexico Statewide Survey Results
Date:September 11, 2025

Key Findings

Democratic primary voters believe young people today will be worse off than previous generations: By a whopping 56-point margin (75% worse off / 19% better off) voters said today's young people will not do as well financially as their parents and grandparents. This pessimistic view is largely consistent with all voters regardless of race, age, party affiliation or parental status. In order to address this concern, voters prioritized improving the quality of schools 2nd (29%) behind only reducing the costs of everyday life like housing and groceries (50%).
Most voters know NM schools are behind the rest of the country: 68% said it was "the lowest or one of the lowest performing states" in math and reading proficiency. Hispanic and Native American voters were much more likely to feel that NM schools were doing better: 33% and 50% respectively said the state was "somewhere in the middle" when compared to other states in math and reading, only 16% of White voters felt this way.
Voters are deeply divided over different visions to improve education: When asked to pick one of three competing approaches towards schools in New Mexico from hypothetical candidates for Governor, voters were split almost evenly between messages that were directed at addressing problems in school directly and one that simply placed the burden for improving outcomes for kids on external factors completely outside of a school's control.
White voters and voters with college degrees are more likely to feel that external factors alone overwhelm schools and improvements are not possible: While voters of color and non-college voters are more likely to feel that improvements within schools are possible and needed, Hispanic voters favored the combined "improve education messages" over "external factors only" by +13 (50% to 27%) Native American voters by +36 (57% to 21%) while "improve education messages" lost out to "external factors only" among white voters by -12 (40% to 52%).
The next Governor should embrace new and proven approaches to prepare young people for success in education and the workforce: Improving access to quality school options including magnet schools, STEM academies, charter schools and tribal compact schools was especially popular with 79% of NM Democrats, including 85% of Hispanic voters and 86% of Native American voters.
An overwhelming majority of New Mexico primary voters support the federal school choice program: 69% of voters favor the Governor opting into the federal tax credit program with 24% opposing. While there's strong support across all age groups and demographics, the strongest support came from voters 18 - 34 years old (79% support), Native Americans (93% support), and voters without a 4-year college degree (75% support).

Methodology

This Concord Public Opinion survey was conducted between 08/30-09/02 with a sample size of 600 respondents. Data was collected by contacting NM likely Democratic primary voters via live calls to cell phones and landlines and Text-to-Web surveys. The margin of error for this survey is +/- 3.94%. Data was weighted by the following: Race, Age, Gender.

Key Findings

A strong majority (65%) approve of how Mayor Reed is handling his job, and would vote to re-elect him (61%) if the August election were held today. Impressively, only a quarter of voters disapprove of his job performance as Mayor, a strikingly low percentage for any Mayor at the end of their first term.
Concord Public Opinion Partners sponsored by Education Reform Now Advocacy (ERNA) recently conducted polling of 263 municipal voters in Montgomery Alabama.

Methodology

The citywide representative poll was conducted by Concord Public Opinion Partners May 10th, 2023, and surveyed municipal voters in Montgomery AL. Data was collected by contacting cell phones and landlines via live callers. The sample size is n=263, with a MOE of +/- 5.89%.
