Exactly as delivered. Demographic columns can't be restated to a different turnout model without joint party-by-demographic tables, which aren't part of this release, so they appear unchanged.

A poll is a sample plus an expectation about who turns out. For every answer, the restated figure is the weighted average of the three published party columns at the expected turnout. Nothing else is changed. No new interviews were conducted and no respondent-level data was used.

Restated % = 0.39 × REP + 0.32 × DEM + 0.29 × NPP
Nothing else changesNo new interviews, no respondent-level data, no reallocation of undecided voters.
Party columns are fixedChanging how many Republicans you expect to vote doesn't change how Republicans answered.
Only the total restatesGender, age and race columns would need joint tables not in this release.
Something we noticed and can't fully explain. Combining the party columns at the expected 39 / 32 / 29 doesn't return the reported toplines. Solving for the shares that do gives a different mix that reproduces every row to four decimal places.

Either the toplines sit at a different party mix, or the crosstab columns are computed on a different weighted base. The second is a reporting artifact and we think it the likelier. We have asked and do not yet have an answer.
Read these before you quote anything here
  1. The fieldwork is KStrat's. Their questionnaire, interviews and reported figures are reproduced exactly as delivered. The restatement is American Encore's.
  2. This is arithmetic on aggregates, not a re-tabulation of respondent-level data, and it doesn't carry the authority of one.
  3. The margin of error still applies. ±3.3 points on the full sample, wider on subgroups. Several differences shown here are smaller than that.
  4. Turnout expectations are contested. 39 / 32 / 29 is an R +7 electorate. Ballots returned by Oct 27 ran 38.6 R to 38.0 D (1,106,723 of 3,286,885), against 36.9 R and 39.4 D at the same point in 2022. Source: Arizona county recorders via Derek Weech's daily series.
