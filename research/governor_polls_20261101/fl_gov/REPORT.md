# Florida governor polling: October 1–November 1, 2026

Ten distinct surveys have all six requested Donalds/Jolly-by-party cells in `polls.json`: RedBox, ActiVote (two independent field periods), Stetson late October, InsiderAdvantage, St. Pete (two waves), UNF, Univision/YouGov, and On Point. This meets the threshold, so no pre-October survey was added. Inclusion is based on publication, not field end; source-document dates and media dates can differ. This is a best-effort inventory, not a claim to have found every possible poll. See `inventory.json` for additional releases and open retrieval or reporting gaps.

The St. Pete figures are explicitly unweighted, with party registration inferred as in the Senate investigation. UNF uses voter-file registration and includes ballot leaners; its governor ballot base is 1,378, not its Senate base. RedBox uses self-identified party and excludes candidate leaners. Stetson uses party identification, a modeled electorate and allocated candidate leaners; the independent base is 82. YouGov's party subgroup bases are **weighted** rather than unweighted counts. Insider's image labels party but does not establish identification versus registration or whether its bases are weighted; it was posted separately from its release, and its own posting date is unresolved. The two ActiVote two-choice polls label party but do not define the basis; group counts remain null rather than converting rounded sample shares into invented counts.

On Point's original linked workbook was exported through the browser and retained in `sources/`. Its registration-party cells are from the governor question (as asked) on the likely-voter tab, not the Senate or allocated-leaner question. The source's likely-voter values use a turnout model applied to the full 601-respondent pool, and the tab reports unweighted bases. Its article and workbook disagree on the overall likely-voter margin of error; subgroup margins should not be inferred from either.

NYT/Siena's original Florida governor table remains unexamined because its content was blocked by browser policy. It is listed as a gap; no alternative retrieval was attempted. Other important gaps include the Stetson mid-October full report (large document not returned by web reader and terminal network unavailable), Big Data's interactive table, and On Point/RedBox older or third-party crosstabs noted in the inventory. The NBC6 Mason-Dixon article gives Democratic Donalds/Jolly figures adding to 104%; the apparent error cannot be resolved from the source and no correction is fabricated. Wedgewood is excluded. Some releases may still become eligible after original governor tables are verified, so the set should not be treated as exhaustive.
