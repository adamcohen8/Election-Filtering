Democrats Lead Republicans 53 – 47 In Congressional Generic Ballot: New John Zogby Strategies Election Day Eve of Election Poll

You are here:
  1. Home
  2. news
  3. Democrats Lead Republicans 53 –…

KEY FINDINGS

 

  1. Consistent with results from congressional districts nationwide, Democrats lead in the national generic ballot for congress by 6 percentage points. In recent history, a Democratic lead 4.5 to 5 percentage points in this national popular reading has been sufficient for the party to gain seats. Note, the Democrats need only a pickup of a net 3 seats to win a majority in the House of Representative.
  2. Democrats hold leads among most key sub-groups displayed in charts below, except white voters, parents of young children at home, and rural voters. Their margin appears to be especially fueled by solid support among Democrats, independents, Hispanics, Blacks, those with college degrees, residents of cities and suburbs, and younger voters.
  3. Democrats slightly benefit regarding strength of support for their candidate with 78% citing strong support vs. 74% of Republicans.
  4. Not surprisingly, and consistent with pre-election surveys, the issue of inflation and affordability dominates as the major driver for voters with about half citing it as their top issue. Healthcare and immigration come in as second and third most cited issues that will determine how voters cast their vote.
  5. The President has campaigned to remind supporters that he is on the ballot. While not technically true, if the race for a majority of Congress is seen as a referendum on Mr. Trump, then he appears to be losing this round.
  6. Of the 5 top cited issues, when asked which party is more likely to deliver on each, Democrats were seen as better on affordability, healthcare, and diplomacy/foreign policy. Republicans lead on immigration and crime.
  7. The poll was conducted online by John Zogby Strategies from November 1-2, 2026 among 1,001 likely voters/already voted and has an overall margin-of-sampling error of +/-3.2 percentage points.

 

Pollster Jeremy Zogby: In the final analysis, both parties had a long list of advantages and disadvantages going into the race, but in the end it appears to come down to the electorate’s preference for a check on the Executive branch and a vote of no confidence regarding the MAGA agenda.
