METHODOLOGY

Echelon Insights conducted a survey on behalf of NetChoice to understand voter perceptions around
antitrust regulations, artificial intelligence, datacenters and the 2026 midterm elections. The survey was
fielded from October 16th to 23rd, 2026 in English among a sample of 3,644 voters in the likely electorate
using a mixed mode approach, with a base sample of 1,004 and oversamples to achieve n=515
respondents in Florida, n=508 in Ohio, n=508 in Georgia, n=403 in Iowa, n=407 in Nebraska and n=413
in Maine. Given some overlap between the oversample and the national base voters, we surveyed a total
of 3,644 respondents, with 1,072 of the respondents in the final sample completing the survey online after
receiving a text message invitation and 2,572 completing the survey online after being drawn from the
web-based sample exchanges [Cint, PureSpectrum and RepData]. The text sample was drawn from the
L2 voter file, and the web sample was matched to the L2 voter file to verify respondents’ voter registration
status.

Measures taken to ensure data quality included measures to prevent duplicate responses, questions
designed to disqualify inattentive respondents, and the removal of respondents from the data file who
answered more than one-third of the questions they were asked in less than one-third of the median
response time per question.

Respondents were asked to indicate their voting preferences in the 2026 midterm election. Those who
had already voted were asked to select their candidate choice from a randomized list of candidates
specific to their state ballot. Respondents who had not yet voted were asked who they would vote for if
the election were held today. Those initially undecided were prompted with a follow-up to indicate a lean
towards a candidate. On the head-to-head ballot, coding was applied to streamline responses into a final
preference by classifying responses as “definitely” for the candidate selected. When asking about the
likelihood of voter participation in the 2026 midterm election, respondents selected from a range of voting
intentions, including options to confirm if they had already cast their vote. Those who indicated they had
already voted specified whether they voted early in person or by mail/absentee, with responses coded to
align with future voting intention categories. Respondents who had not yet voted detailed their voting
plans, with all responses coded according to method or uncertainty regarding voting.

The sample was weighted to reflect modeled turnout and demographic characteristics of the population of
voters in the 2026 likely electorate based on a probabilistic model that incorporates data from the US
Census Bureau’s American Community Survey and Current Population Survey Voting and Registration
Supplement, as well as L2 voter file data. Weighting dimensions included gender, age, race/ethnicity,
education, designated market area (DMA), turnout probability, party by ideology, as well as gender by
age.

The oversamples for Florida, Ohio, Georgia, Iowa, Nebraska and Maine were weighted down to the
proportion in the population of voters in the likely electorate in those states.

Adjusted to incorporate the effect of weighting, the overall margin of sampling error is ± 3.2 percentage
points. Margin of error was ± 5.0 percentage points for Florida, ± 5.2 percentage points for Ohio, ± 5.2
percentage points for Georgia, ± 6.5 percentage points for Iowa, ± 6.3 percentage points for Nebraska
and ± 6.3 percentage points for Maine.
