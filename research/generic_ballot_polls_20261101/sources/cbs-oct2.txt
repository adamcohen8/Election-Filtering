       HOW THE POLL WAS CONDUCTED AND THE
              MARGIN OF ERROR WAS CALCULATED






The CBS News/YouGov survey of 2,166 adults in the U.S. was conducted between September
30–October 2, 2026.  Respondents were selected to be representative of adults nationwide.
This sample was weighted according to gender, age, race, education, and 2024 presidential vote.
Weighting targets come from the U.S. Census American Community Survey and the U.S. Census
Current Population Survey.  “Likely voters” – respondents’ relative probabilities of voting – were
determined using a regression model that incorporates current, self-reported intentions to vote,
along  with  individual-level  and  aggregate  data  about  voters,  such  as  demographics  and  vote
history.  The weights range from 0.1 to 5.0, with a mean of 1 and a standard deviation of 0.63.
The margin of error  (a 95% confidence interval) for a sample percentage p based upon the entire
sample is approximately ±2.5 points.  It is calculated using the formula
                                                       s
                                               100 ×      1 + CV2
                                                              n

where CV is the coefficient of variation of the sample weights and n is the sample size used to
compute the proportion.  This is a measure of sampling error.  The sample estimate should differ
from  its  expected  value  –  the  average  of  all  estimates  obtained  by  repeating  the  same  sample
selection and weighting procedures – by less than the margin of error in 95 percent of all samples.
It does not reflect non-sampling errors, including potential selection bias in panel participation or
in response to a particular survey.
The estimated numbers of Republican and Democratic seats come from a statistical model de-
veloped  by  CBS  News  and  YouGov,  which  includes  data  from  this  poll,  past  vote  and  other
district-level characteristics,  demographics and historical patterns.  These estimates are subject
to  statistical  error.   We  simulate  many  elections,  with  the  error  calibrated  to  include  historical
polling accuracy, to obtain a range of seat estimates.  We report 95% confidence intervals from the
simulations.  These estimates describe which party is ahead today and may change as additional
data are collected closer to the election.
