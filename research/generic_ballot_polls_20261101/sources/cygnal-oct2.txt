Insights & Analysis

The Ballot and the Electorate (vs. September)
–  Democrats lead the generic ballot 52% to 43%, holding D+9 for a second month with the
   highest Democratic share this cycle. Undecideds dropped to 6%, and they broke leftward.
      –  Independents moved from D+24 in September to D+28 as their undecided share fell
         from 21% to 14%. The Independent movement is nearly all Center Independents.
         D48→62 since July. Right-Lean Independents have slipped R81→66.
      –  The gender gap widened to 30 points. Women moved from D+21 to D+23, while men
         held at R+7.
      –  Non-college voters held steady at D+2 (D+3 in September), while college graduates
         moved from D+14 to D+17, and voters under 55 from D+19 to D+22.
      –  February (R+1) was the last time this cycle the GOP held an edge on the generic ballot.
–  Undecided voters are disengaged, not torn: only 26% are high news attention (vs 45%
   overall), 56% highly motivated (vs 83%), 44% say their vote matters (vs 64%), and 67% are
   frustrated with both parties. 59% Ind, 53% moderate. Trump image of 18/69. Top issues:
   inflation 31, gov’t spending 17. Media sources: free streaming 24% vs 14% overall, YouTube
   29% vs 23%, cable news 20% vs 32%.
–  Asked what mix of control voters want in DC, they split 42% to 38% between full Democratic
   and full Republican control, with 10% preferring divided government. Independents favor a
   Democratic sweep 43-27.

Insights & Analysis

      –  Swing voters break the other way. 30% prefer full Republican control, 23% full
         Democratic control, 20% a split, and 28% are unsure. Their ballot lean is softer than it
         looks.
–  Swing voters, the soft partisans and undecideds, are 23% of the electorate. 67% say the
   economy is getting worse, 40% are frustrated with both parties, and only 40% are extremely
   motivated –    a challenging mix for the incumbent party.
–  Double Haters (of both parties) (18% of voters)           vote D+29 (31R-60D-9U). They are 78%
   Trump unfav and 72% want a check on him. Their profile is upscale: 42% Ind, 56% $100k+,
   57% college, 56% men. The R-ballot third are fiscal voters (spending 25, inflation 41) and
   87% stay R on a break.
–  Voters now expect a Democratic Congress by 15 points (51-36), up from D+12 in September
   and D+2 in August. Swing voters expect Democrats to win by 16, up from 2 in August, and
   Republicans are now -7 net.
      –  Fatalist Republicans (Trump/GOP voters who expect a Dem win) are 9% of voters
         (about 1 in 5 on the R side). Only 71% vote GOP (vs 97%), 49% definitely R, 31% say
         their vote matters nationally, and only 46% extremely motivated. Inflation is their top
         issue at 45% (vs 22%). They are a huge turnout risk and need a believable pocketbook
         message.
–  Democrats hold a 10-point enthusiasm edge, 74% extremely motivated to 64% of
   Republicans. Trump Republicans (74%) are 7 points less motivated than Progressive
   Democrats; traditional conservative Republicans trail at 58%.

Insights & Analysis

      –  Asked what is driving their vote, opposing Trump is the largest single vote driver at 25%
         (+2 since April), while voting to support Trump is down 4 points.
      –  Vote driver by ballot strength: Soft Rs vote for Republican ideals (47%), not Trump
         (13%). Soft Ds: oppose Trump 38%, frustrated 33% at everyone. Undecideds: 67%
         frustrated.
–  Trump's image held at -19 net for a second month after being -13 in August. Republicans in
   Congress gained +2 to -16, while Democrats in Congress slipped -4 to -11, shrinking the
   Democratic brand edge from 11 points to 5.
      –  Comparing back to March, Trump’s image (fav/unfav) has fallen among Rs +75→+67,
         women -23→-34, age 18-29 -40→-61, moderates -40→-52, Hispanics -27→-41, and
         undecideds -29→-51.
      –  In February, Trump’s net image was just -2, but it has been on a largely steady decline
         since.
–  Chuck Schumer is a growing weak spot for the Democrats at -33 net fav, down 12 since
   October 2025. His net among Democrats fell from +13 in August to +6, while Mamdani sits at
   +58 net fav from Democrats (only -7 overall).
      –  Schumer works as a villain with Double Haters (74% unfav), Right-Lean Indeps                   (75%),
         and soft Rs (65%), but not with undecideds or soft Dems. Mamdani works with the base
         (79% R unfav) and Right-Lean Inds          (75%).

Insights & Analysis

How the Environment Compares to October 2022 and 2024
–  The generic ballot (D+9) has moved 11 points toward Democrats since October 2022 and
   October 2024, both 49 R –        47 D. The largest driver is the absence of Biden as President and
   Trump's voters cooling on him a year and a half into his second term.
–  Among those who backed Trump in the presidential, favorability fell from 92% in October
   2024 to 83%, and very favorable from 69% to 54%. Their congressional vote slipped from 96-
   3 Republican to 90-5.
      –  That erosion moves the national margin about 4 points. Third-party presidential voters
         and 2024 non-voters breaking Democratic add about 4 more. Another 3 points come
         from fewer voters claiming Trump at all: 47% said they were voting for him in October
         2024, but 44% now say they voted for him.
–  The souring is sharpest at the edges of his coalition, not the core base. Trump-voting
   Independents fell from R+82 to R+57, moderates from R+79 to R+61, and voters under 55
   from R+89 to R+75.
      –  Core Republicans and conservatives cooled in intensity more than loyalty, which means
         GOP candidates are likely to have a turnout issue this cycle.
      –  This is exacerbated by a slide in the Trump-type share of Republicans, 45% to 42%
         since July (inside noise overall), with real drops among Republicans under $50K (60 to
         41), ages 18-44 (49 to 35), and 65+ (50 to 39). Traditional Republicans are now 56% of
         Republicans, up from 51%.Very favorable fell 14 points among Republican Trump voters
         and 16 among conservatives, yet their ballot support sits at 96% (-3) and 94% (-5).

Insights & Analysis

–  The swing is almost entirely female. Women went from D+4 in 2022 and D+9 in 2024 to
   D+23, while men sat at R+7, R+12, and R+7.
      –  Since 2024, women under 55 moved from D+13 to D+35, and men under 55 flipped
         from R+8 to D+7. Men 55+ went the other way, from R+18 to R+21.
      –  Non-college women moved from R+5 in both October 2022 and 2024 to D+16.
         Moderate women went from D+23 in 2022 to D+50, and Black women from D+55 in
         2024 to D+79.
–  The national mood is actually better than either prior October (37% right direction –              60%
   wrong track now, 33-64 in 2022, 33-61 in 2024), but wrong-track voters have completely
   flipped on the generic ballot from R+45 in 2024 to D+67.
      –  Partisans swapped places as the party in power flipped: Republicans saying right
         direction jumped from 11% to 76% since Biden's final year in 2024, Democrats fell from
         61% to 8% as Trump took over. Independents are sourer than they've been in either
         prior October at 24 -    72.
–  Trump's image fell from 47 fav, 51 unfav in October 2024 to 39 fav, 58 unfav, a -15 net slide.
   Most of it came from voters under 55 (-23 net); voters 55+ slipped 9 points net, from 51-48 to
   46-52.
      –  Independents went from -21 to -39, women under 55 from -19 to -46, and moderates
         from -33 to -52. Men 55+ held steady, from 55-44 to 56-42.

Insights & Analysis

–  Trump's 2024 coalition has frayed more than defected. 93% of his voters would choose him
   again, 3% would switch to Harris, and 3% are unsure. His share fell from 47% to 44%; Harris
   held at 50%.
      –  The voters who pulled the Trump lever and now would vote for Harris or are undecided
         (3% of electorate) are 55% Independent, inflation is their top issue by a whopping 57%,
         88% say the economy is worse, 74% want a check on Trump, and 66% expect a D win.
      –  Independents moved from Harris +3 in October 2024 to Harris +22 (33-55), with 12%
         now undecided.
–  Voters feel powerless compared to 2024. Those saying their vote makes a big difference
   nationally fell from 43% to 26%: Democrats 50% to 26%, Republicans 45% to 33%,
   Independents 33% to 16%, and under-55 voters 37% to 19%.
      –  Republicans got more establishment-minded once in power. Those preferring traditional
         candidates rose from 17% to 25% since 2024, while shake-up voters fell from 17% to
         10%. Reform-minded candidates gained overall, 24% to 28%.
Economy and the Ad Wars, Then and Now
–  Inflation jumped +5 to 32% as Congress's top priority this month, +10 since July. Voters under
   55 rank it first at 40% versus 26% of those 55+.
      –  The issue agenda flipped by party. Inflation sits at 32%, near October 2024 (34%) and
         below 2022 (41%), but Democrats now prioritize it over Republicans 36% to 27%,
         reversing 2024's 25% to 42%.

Insights & Analysis

      –  Republicans drove it. Traditional Republicans rose from 21% to 32%, as did ages 30                -44
         (35% to 45%) and 65+ (17% to 26%). Republicans naming inflation still vote 90% R, but
         their Trump unfavorability    rose from 17% to 26%.
–  Abortion collapsed as a priority, from 13% in 2022 and 9% in 2024 to 2%; among Democrats,
   from 21% to 1%. Republican focus on immigration fell from 31% to 23% since 2024.
      –  59% say the economy is getting worse, nearly matching October 2022 (61%), but the
         partisans swapped places: Democrats moved -51 points worse, Republicans +61 better.
–  Total gas pain barely moved since 2022 (66% to 68% hurting), but rising-and-hurting jumped
   up 14 points. Republicans feeling hurt fell -28 and Democrats rose +28. Gas is up for 82% of
   Independents, a number that was only 57% in 2022.
–  2024 was an immigration and abortion ad cycle (63% and 54% ad recall). 2026 is a gas
   (44%) and Iran war (33%) cycle, with immigration down to 28% and abortion to 5%.
      –  This year's ads reach every partisan group about equally: gas recall is 43% among
         Democrats, 46% among Republicans, and 44% among Independents, with Iran war at
         33/34/32. In 2024, immigration recall split 71% Republican to 55% Democratic.
      –  Inflation ad recall shows the same partisan flip as the economy, falling -15 among
         Republicans (58% to 43%) and rising +3 among Democrats (47% to 50%).
      –  Abortion recall collapsed hardest where it was strongest: Democrats -51 (57% to 6%)
         and women -52 (58% to 6%). The top Democratic message of 2024 has nearly
         vanished.

Insights & Analysis

      –  Healthcare is the one topic gaining with Democrats, +5 to 17% overall, led by women
         (21% versus 12% of men) and Democrats (21%). Transgender ads rose +4 to 15%, led
         by Republicans at 18%.
      –  Price ads dominate what persuadable voters recall: 57% of probably                -D voters and 54%
         of Center Independents recall inflation ads, and 52% of probably-R voters recall gas
         ads. Government spending, a top issue for soft Republican-side voters, isn't on the ad
         list.
–  Ads are breaking through less. Voters recall 2.1 topics on average versus 2.7 in 2024, and
   unsure tripled from 3% to 10%. Some of that is a midterm carrying less advertising than a
   presidential year.
The Trump Distance Test
–  When a Republican “publicly        disagreed    with President Trump      on a topic or issue”, the generic
   ballot tightens from D+9 to essentially tied (45-44-11), a 10-point net gain for the GOP, though
   most movers go to undecided rather than to the Republican.
      –  Independents shift from D+28 to D+4 and swing voters from D+4 to R+18. Men under
         55 flip from D+7 to R+7, and college men from D+1 to R+12.
–  The cost with GOP voters is real but small. Trump Republicans' GOP share dips from 96% to
   91%, and 10% of Trump-favorable voters go unsure. Traditional Republicans slip 4 points on
   the congressional ballot. Meanwhile, 29% of soft Democrats cross over to Republicans.

Insights & Analysis

–  The Iran war is where voters most want Republicans to break with Trump (36%), followed by
   corruption (26%), tariffs (24%), and healthcare (23%). Iran leads among swing voters at 42%.
      –  Only 46% of Republicans say their members should stand with Trump on everything.
         55% of traditional conservatives name at least one break issue, led by Iran at 21%. 64%
         of Trump Republicans name none.
      –  Iran is also where a break pays off most. Voters who name it move from D+56 to D+35
         when a Republican disagrees with Trump
      –  Tariffs are the second-best break. Tariff voters shift from D+45 to D+25, while breaks on
         corruption (D+52 to D+39) and healthcare (D+57 to D+43) move voters about half as
         much.
–  A second tightening test was also conducted asking what would happen if a “socialist
   Democrat” was running, and it ties the ballot 45-45-10. 9% move on each test and 5% on
   both. Most movers go undecided, not Republican (break: 5R/4U; socialist: 3R/6U). Dem
   voters hold 83% on both.
–  Movers are 45% Independent, 50-55% moderate, about 60% women, 65-67% Harris voters,
   about 90% Trump unfav. Not really a group that’s clamouring              to vote GOP.
–  Voters want a check on Trump’s administration over a rubber stamp by a 20-point margin (36-
   56). Independents prefer a check 68-23, swing voters 61-24, and 25% of traditional
   conservative Republicans agree.
      –  16% of GOP-ballot voters want a check on the President.

Insights & Analysis

      –  10% want a check on Trump but aren't voting Democratic (7% R, 3% undecided). They
         are 55% Republican, 69% Trump 2024 voters, and 53% conservative, with inflation
         (37%) and spending (16%) on top. Lukewarm Republicans, not Democrats in waiting.
What Can Republicans Do to Turn This Around?
–  Lead with price empathy and plan, not Trump. Inflation is the issue that moved (27% to
   32%), and it moved among Republicans. Soft Republicans vote for Republican ideals (47%),
   not for Trump (13%). Traditional Republicans (21% of voters) are the most at-risk Republican
   group: 73% definitely R and inflation up 12 points among them since July.
–  Be willing to show independence on Iran and tariffs. Those two breaks draw the most
   interest from the 9% who tighten the ballot and from the check-but-not-D voters (10% of
   voters, 69% voted Trump), and they cost little with Trump Republicans (10% and 9%).
   Immigration, deportations, and healthcare breaks mainly appeal to locked-in Democrats.
–  Fix the fatalism. 9% of voters are Trump voters or GOP voters who expect to lose, and only
   31% of them think their vote matters nationally. "A big difference" nationally fell from 43% to
   26% since Oct 2024. A message about why their vote counts reaches the group that will
   otherwise stay home or go soft.
–  Reach Right-Lean and Center Independents as balance voters. They are strategic voters
   (41% and 37%), so a check-and-balance frame fits. Right-Lean Independents are slipping (R
   81% to 66% since July, small cell). Center Independents are mostly gone (D 62%), so spend
   there only if the budget allows.

Insights & Analysis

–  Use Schumer and Mamdani for the base and soft Republicans, not for undecideds.
   Undecideds and soft Democrats mostly do not know Schumer (31% and 32%).
–  Buy the right mix of media. Undecideds and the drifting Trump voters live on free
   streaming, YouTube, and websites. Double Haters over-index on podcasts. Cable news
   reaches the base (Trump Republicans 41%, Traditional Republicans 40%), not the swing.
–  Do not lean on an economy-is-better message. Independents are as negative as in 2022
   (69% worse), and gas pain now lands on the party in power: voters hurt by rising gas vote
   D+32 now, against R+39 in 2022.
Latent Class Analysis Voter Segments
–  Trump Republicans (26%): 99% Republican, 95% favorable to Trump, and 87% right
   direction. Immigration (33%) tops their priorities, and 59% want Republicans to stand with
   Trump on everything.
      –  65% are 55+, 86% white, and 72% married. 53% live in rural areas or small towns.
–  Soft Republicans (18%): 88% Republican, but only 74% favorable to Trump. They vote on
   Republican ideals (55%) over Trump (13%), and 15% aren't very motivated, the GOP's
   turnout risk.
      –  57% are men, 37% call themselves independents, and 30% are moderates.
–  True Swing/Independents (16%): 67% Democratic with 27% undecided. A Trump-breaking
   Republican moves them from D+61 to D+17, and 46% want a break on the Iran war.

Insights & Analysis

     –   62% are under 55, 21% Black, and 58% moderate. 44% voted in two or fewer of the last
         four generals.
–  Committed Democrats (40%): unanimous Democratic ballot and 96% motivated. Opposing
   Trump (49%) edges Democratic ideals (46%) as their vote reason, yet they split 41-40 on
   Schumer.
     –   64% are women, 65% liberal, and 53% college          -educated.
