                                                           Methodology in Brief
YouGov interviewed 1045 respondents who were then matched down to a sample of 1000 to produce the final dataset.
The respondents were matched to a sampling frame on gender, age, race, and education. The sampling frame was
constructed by stratified sampling       from the 2024 Current Population Survey (CPS) Voting and Registration supplements,
and the 2024 AP Votecast exit poll, including demographics and 2024 presidential vote. All data were weighted to the
CPS-VRS person weights on the public use file, with Hur            & Achen reweighting adjustments to account for missing data
within the voter registration and turnout fields. The matched cases were then iteratively proportionally fitted (IPF)                    on
2024 presidential vote choice. Additionally, weighted results were then compared to expected proportions for likely
voter profiles given a battery of three items in order to         slightly increase weights for likely voters than their unlikely voter
counterparts. These included, 1) how closely they follow news about candidates running during the 2024 election; 2)
How often they say that they vote when there is a midterm election; a              nd 3) how likely they are to vote in the election in
November. Where the data were lower then              expected, they were upweighted. The final component of the IPF consisted
of a four-way stratification of gender, age (4      -categories), race (4   -categories), and education (4      -categories), and these
demographics' associated marginals, in addition to region and home ownership, to produce
the final weight

UMass Lowell is a public institution and releases surveys as a source of public
information.  We offer a complete methodology report in adherence with standards of the
AAPOR Transparency Initiative, as well an extensive set of crosstabs.              Our goal is complete
transparency in the reporting of our findings.         If there is something you do not see here, but
wish you did, please contact Director of Survey Research, Dr. John Cluverius
(john_cluverius@uml.edu;        @JohnCluverius).
