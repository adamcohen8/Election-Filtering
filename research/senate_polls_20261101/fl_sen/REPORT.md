# Florida U.S. Senate polling, publication October 1–November 1, 2026

Search covered 270toWin, RealClearPolling, On Point Politics, Wikipedia's election poll table, Florida Atlantic's polling archive, targeted searches for CBS/YouGov, NYT/Siena and Big Data Poll, and original reports where available. The inventory has 24 candidate releases or explicit exclusions, including one blocked fabricated-pollster entry; this is an auditable search, not proof that every internet release was found. Publication dates and field dates are distinct. Null means not verified. Original PDF reports were read with the web PDF text interface; centrally downloaded copies exist under `research/senate_polls_20261101/verification/sources/` for the six original rows. Precise source links and table excerpts accompany every record.

Seven releases have complete candidate-by-party or candidate-by-registration tables, in `polls.json`. Four are ready: RedBox November 1 (party ID), UNF October 21 and St. Pete October 7 and 27 (registration proxies). Three require review: Stetson, Univision/YouGov, and Mason-Dixon. All seven have reported subgroup bases; Univision's are weighted rather than respondent counts. Ballot leaners and party leaners are separate concepts.

RedBox's first-choice ballot excludes candidate leaners and uses unleaned party ID: its broad independent group includes independent, another party, and don't know, regardless of registration. Its registration crosstab is not counted separately. The release reports overall weighting design effect 1.37; party subgroup margins of error are R ±3.3, D ±3.8, and I ±4.1 points, distinct from overall ±2.1, per the source's Q2/Q3 table. UNF includes ballot leaners and combines NPA/other voter-file registrations into the independent proxy. Its table base 1,414 excludes refusals and those who would not vote; subgroup bases are unweighted. St. Pete's tables explicitly report unweighted results; registration is inferred from the voter-file sampling frame, party weighting, no reported party-identification question, and the exact match between the Independent breakdown count and demographic Other count. Other registrations are mapped to the independent proxy; specific constituent parties are not enumerated.

Stetson explicitly labels its n=83 independent group party identification, which need not equal the 245 NPA/other registration composition; that difference alone is not a contradiction. The party question and treatment of party leaners remain unspecified in the available PDF, and the small subgroup warrants review. Estimates model likely voters over registered voters, allocate candidate leaners, and include reported votes. Univision/YouGov's report calls its table “Full Recoded” and gives weighted party bases. A sponsor Univision broadcast identifies Nixon 42% and Moody 51%, corroborating the generic Democratic and Republican candidate rows, but the handling of party and candidate leaners and suitability of weighted n remain unresolved. Mason-Dixon's sponsor NBC6 indexed article text supplies complete candidate support and exact subgroup counts; its Independent registration bucket is mapped as reported, but the composition of other registrations is not enumerated and direct article access returned 403. The embedded poll document was not read; retain for review. A separate FloridaPolitics account describes the sample as independent or other.

The October 25 RedBox wave was indexed with field dates October 20–23 and n=4,823, but the live original path now displays the November 1 wave and its historical party table was not recovered. Big Data Poll's original headline and opening name Angie Craig, whereas its body names Angie Nixon; its interactive table did not yield usable extracted rows, and sample counts differ among sources. NYT/Siena links directly to its Florida crosstabs, but retrieval returned a non-retryable error; the field-end date differs across listings (October 25 versus 26). Targeted searches did not identify a separate CBS-sponsored Florida Senate wave; the CBS/YouGov wave found in the same period was Texas. Other exclusions, including missing tables, unknown publication dates, and a September release, appear in `inventory.json`. Wedgewood is explicitly excluded.
