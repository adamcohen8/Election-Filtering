 Klobuchar Remains 7 Points Ahead of Demuth for MN Governor, as Other DFL Candidates Gain Back Some Ground, But 3 Key Races Remain Tight:
                                                                                                             
 One month after showing Republican candidates on the upswing and 3 weeks until Election Day, SurveyUSA's latest exclusive polling for KSTP-TV Minneapolis, KAAL-TV Rochester, and
 WDIO-TV Duluth finds DFL candidates regaining the lead (if only nominally) in three contests, widening a lead in another, and holding steady in the fifth polled contest.
                                                                                                             
 In the election to replace Tim Walz as Governor , DFL candidate Amy Klobuchar today is at 48%, Republican Lisa Demuth at 41% &ndash; both up one point from last month's tracking , leaving
 Klobuchar's 7-point lead unchanged. Green Party candidate Steven Young remains at 3%; undecideds dip from 9% to 8%. Demuth now leads by a nominal point among men, up from a one-point
 deficit, while Klobuchar widens her lead among women from 14 to 17 points. 86% of Republicans vote for Demuth; 90% of Democrats vote for Klobuchar; independents give Klobuchar a 3-point
 advantage, 42% to 39%. While it's close among independents, moderates continue to strongly back Klobuchar, though by a narrower 23-point margin than the 31 to 32 point margin seen in
 previous polling .                                                                                          
 In the contest for the US Senate currently held by Tina Smith, DFL candidate Peggy Flanagan gains back 3 points and Republican Michele Tafoya holds steady month-on-month , giving Flanagan
 the edge, 45% to 42%, with 2% each voting for one of the third-party candidates in the race and 9% undecided. Tafoya was at 41% in August, at 42% in both September and today; Flanagan was
 at 46% in August, 42% last month, and at 45% today. Tafoya leads by 5 points among men, Flanagan by 11 points among women &ndash; a 16-point gender gap. Independents here favor the
 Republican by a 5-point margin, down from 14 points last month, while moderates again prefer the DFL candidate, here by 11 points, down from 15 . Tafoya's lead in rural parts of the state has
 risen from 19 points to 35 points; that's offset by Flanagan's urban advantage increasing from 6 points to 37 and a modest improvement for the DFL candidate in the suburbs.
                                                                                                             
 Any outcome remains possible in the very close and recently quite bouncy race for Attorney General , where incumbent DFL candidate Keith Ellison today is back in the lead, though nominally,
 with 45% to Republican Ron Schutz's 44%. One month ago , Schutz led by 2 points; two months ago, the candidates were tied; prior to that, during the primary season, Ellison was consistently
 ahead by margins of 7, 4, and 8 points. Today, Schutz leads by 10 points among men, Ellison by 12 among women, a gender gap of 22 points, notably wider than the 12-point gap seen last
 month. Voters under age 50 favor Ellison by 10 points, up from 7 points last month; those 50+ back Schutz by 8, down slightly from 9 last month. Ellison appears to be making inroads among
 independents , where he had trailed by 10, now trails by 6 &ndash; while Schutz has done the same with moderates , where he trailed by 14 points last month but by 8 today.
 Both the Republican challenger and the DFL incumbent Secretary of State have gained ground in the Secretary of State contest, where Steve Simon (DFL) now leads Tad Jude (R) by 6 points,
 43% to 37%, with 3% voting for Libertarian George "Chip" Tangen and 2% for Green Party candidate Seth Kuhl-Stennes; 15% are undecided, down from 20% in September. Simon is up 3 points,
 Jude up 1. Simon has a consistent 10-point lead among voters under age 50, while those 50+ effectively remain tied , with Simon one nominal point ahead. Independents and moderates both
 favor Simon here, by margins of 6 and 20 points respectively.                                               
                                                                                                             
 Finally, in the race for State Auditor , where last month Republican Scott Jensen was 7 points atop DFL candidate Zack Filipovich, today Filipovich is a nominal point ahead of Jensen, 41% to 40%,
 with 5% voting for Forward Independence candidate Jay Reeves and 14% undecided. Filipovich has widened his lead among women from 3 points to 9, and reduced the gap among men , where
 he had trailed by 15, now trails by 7. Moderates here favor the DFL candidate, by 16 points, up from 7 points in September; among independents, Jensen had led by 13 , but today the contest is
 tied.                                                                                                       
                                                                                                             
 Some month-on-month changes to the list of most important issues offered to voters produce no changes in the three that most matter &ndash; the only three with double-digits selecting them as
 the most important to their votes this year. 36% say inflation / the cost of living / gas prices is most important to their vote (gas prices, a standalone issue last month, is now combined with these
 related topics); those voters choose the DFL candidate in each contest, by margins ranging from 23 to 39 points. The 18% who say fraud is the most important issue consistently favor the
 Republican, by margins ranging from 75 to 87 points. 11% choose healthcare as the most important issue; these voters back the DFL candidate in each race, by margins of 37 to 46 points.

 About the Research / Filtering: SurveyUSA interviewed 900 adults from the state of Minnesota 10/08/26 through 10/12/26 using nonprobability sample of internet panel members provided by Cint

 USA. Of the adults, 818 were identified as being registered to vote; of the registered voters, 718 were identified as being probably or definitely likely to vote in the November general election and
 asked the substantive questions which follow. The combined pool of survey respondents was weighted to US Census ACS targets for gender, age, race, education, and home ownership. In an
 alternate scenario , SurveyUSA also included less-likely voters &ndash; the 9% of registered voters who said they might or might not vote in November &ndash; in the likely voter universe. Adding
 those less-likely voters, as in the past, increases the number of undecided voters; it increases Amy Klobuchar's lead for Governor from 7 points to 9; it increases Keith Ellison's lead for Attorney
 General from 1 point to 3; it increases Steve Simon's lead for Secretary of State from 6 points to 7; and it increases Zack Filipovich's Auditor lead from 1 point to 3. Peggy Flanagan's overall 3-point
 lead in the race for US Senate does not shift.

 Statement of Methodology: SurveyUSA is an independent, non-partisan, apolitical research company that conducts opinion surveys for media, academic institutions, commercial clients,
 non-profits, governments, agencies, and elected officials. SurveyUSA opinion research is conducted using a methodology optimized for each particular project. In some cases, this means data is
 collected 100% by telephone; in some cases, 100% online; and in other cases, a blend of the two. For those projects that are conducted “mixed-mode” (or “multi-mode”): Respondents who have a
 home (landline) telephone are interviewed by phone, sometimes using live interviewers, other times using the recorded voice of a professional announcer. The youngest male is requested on
 approximately 30% of calls to home phones, the youngest adult is requested on approximately 70% of calls. This method of intra-household selection reduces the potential for age and gender
 imbalance in the unweighted sample. Re-attempts are made to busy signals, no-answers, and answering machines. Landline telephone sample is purchased from Aristotle of Washington DC.
 Respondents who do not use a home telephone are interviewed on an electronic device, which means, for some projects, that call-center employees hand-dial cell phones and interview
 respondents verbally on the respondent’s cell phone, and means, for other projects, that SurveyUSA displays the questions visually on the respondent’s phone, tablet, or other device. Sample for
 respondents who do not use a home telephone is purchased from Aristotle or from one of several other research companies that provide access to cell respondents. Where meaningful,
 SurveyUSA indicates the percentage of respondents who use a home phone and the percentage who do not, and crosstabs by this distinction. Responses are minimally weighted to U.S. Census
 or voter file targets for gender, age and race. Target (cell) weighting is used. On questionnaires that ask about political party identification, SurveyUSA may or may not weight to Party ID, depending
 on client preference. Where necessary, questions and answer choices are rotated to prevent order bias, recency, and latency effects. On some studies, certain populations are over-sampled, so
 that the number of unweighted respondents exceeds the number of weighted respondents. Each individual SurveyUSA release contains the date(s) on which interviews are conducted and a
 release date. If interviewing for a particular study is conducted in Spanish, or in any other foreign language, it will be noted on the specific release. If no notation appears, interviews are conducted
