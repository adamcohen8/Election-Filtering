One Month Until Early Voting Begins, Republicans Gain Ground, DFL Falls Back in Statewide Minnesota Contests; Senate Race Now Dead Even:
Republican candidates are on the upswing, according to SurveyUSA's latest exclusive polling for KSTP-TV Minneapolis, KAAL-TV Rochester, and WDIO-TV Duluth. While DFL candidates remain
in the lead for Governor and Secretary of State, Republicans now have a lead in the race for State Auditor, an edge in the Attorney General race, and have tied up the race to replace Tina Smith in
the United States Senate.
Looking first at the election for Governor , today SurveyUSA shows DFL candidate Amy Klobuchar at 47%, Republican Lisa Demuth at 40%; Green Party candidate Steve Young is at 3%; 9% of
likely voters are undecided. Compared to one month ago , Klobuchar is down 5 points, Demuth is up 4. There is notable movement toward Demuth among both men, where Klobuchar had led by
10 points, now leads by 1, and among independent voters, where Klobuchar had led by 17, now leads by 1. Among voters age 50 to 64 , Klobuchar had led by 12, now trails by 6. Klobuchar is
helped by double-digit leads in both the greater Twin Cities and in Northeastern Minnesota, by her 31-point lead among moderates, and by the fact she isn't trailing among independents, which is
not the case for most of the other DFL candidates in our polling today.
The US Senate race is now tied, with Republican Michele Tafoya and DFL candidate Peggy Flanagan each at 42%, and two third-party candidates, Libertarian Rebecca Whiting and independent
Marisa Simonetti, each at 2%; 12% of likely voters say they are undecided. Since last month , Tafoya has gained 1 point and Flanagan has lost 4 points. Tafoya has increased her lead among 50
to 64-year-olds from 5 to 15 points, which is somewhat offset by Flanagan closing her gap against Tafoya from 8 points to 2 points among voters 65+ . Tafoya is leading by 83 points among
Republicans, while Flanagan is leading by 83 points among Democrats &ndash; while independents break for the Republican by a 14-point margin, 43% to 29%. One month ago, Tafoya led by a
single point among independent voters.
Because the candidates for Attorney General were both effectively known before the primary election took place, SurveyUSA has polled this contest five times over the past three month, rather
than the two polls conducted for each of the other offices. Incumbent DFL candidate Keith Ellison led Republican Ron Schutz in the first three polls, then was tied with him last month; today, Schutz
gains 2 points as Ellison holds steady, putting the Republican ahead, 45% to 43%, with 12% of likely voters undecided. Ellison, first elected Attorney General in 2018 after serving 6 terms in
Congress, has consistently polled between 42% and 44%; Schutz, a trial attorney, initially ranged between 36% and 38% before climbing to tie Ellison at 43% last month and now to the lead at
45%.
For Secretary of State , incumbent DFL candidate Steve Simon had led by 7 points last month , now leads by 4, taking 40% against Republican Tad Jude, at 36%. 3% say they will vote for Green
Party candidate Seth Kuhl-Stennes; 2% for Libertarian George Tangen, while 20% are undecided. Jude is up 2 points from a month ago, Simon down 1. In last month's polling, SurveyUSA did not
explicitly name the third-party candidates in this contest, where "another candidate" took a combined 4% of the vote, vs. the combined 5% we see today. The high number of undecideds (steady at
20%), combined with the relatively lower profile of this race means any outcome will be possible.
Finally, looking further down-ballot to the open-seat election for State Auditor , Republican Scott Jensen today leads DFL candidate Zach Filipovich by a 7-point margin, 43% to 36%, with 4%
saying they will vote for Forward Independence candidate Jay Reeves, and 17% saying they are undecided. last month , Jensen and Filipovich were tied at 39%, with 3% voting for a then
unnamed third-party candidate, and 19% undecided. (Parenthetically, SurveyUSA observes that naming third-party candidates, especially in down-ballot contests that many voters are not closely
following, tends to over-state their actual performance on election day.)

About the Research / Filtering: SurveyUSA interviewed 900 adults from the state of Minnesota 09/09/26 through 09/14/26 using nonprobability sample of internet panel members provided by
Cint USA. Of the adults, 800 were identified as being registered to vote; of the registered voters, 654 were identified as being probably or definitely likely to vote in the November general election
and asked the substantive questions which follow. The combined pool of survey respondents was weighted to US Census ACS targets for gender, age, race, education, and home ownership. In
an alternate scenario , SurveyUSA also included less-likely voters &ndash; the 12% of registered voters who said they might or might not vote in November &ndash; in the likely voter universe.

Statement of Methodology: SurveyUSA is an independent, non-partisan, apolitical research company that conducts opinion surveys for media, academic institutions, commercial clients,
non-profits, governments, agencies, and elected officials. SurveyUSA opinion research is conducted using a methodology optimized for each particular project. In some cases, this means data is
collected 100% by telephone; in some cases, 100% online; and in other cases, a blend of the two. For those projects that are conducted “mixed-mode” (or “multi-mode”): Respondents who have a
home (landline) telephone are interviewed by phone, sometimes using live interviewers, other times using the recorded voice of a professional announcer. The youngest male is requested on
approximately 30% of calls to home phones, the youngest adult is requested on approximately 70% of calls. This method of intra-household selection reduces the potential for age and gender
imbalance in the unweighted sample. Re-attempts are made to busy signals, no-answers, and answering machines. Landline telephone sample is purchased from Aristotle of Washington DC.
Respondents who do not use a home telephone are interviewed on an electronic device, which means, for some projects, that call-center employees hand-dial cell phones and interview
respondents verbally on the respondent’s cell phone, and means, for other projects, that SurveyUSA displays the questions visually on the respondent’s phone, tablet, or other device. Sample for
respondents who do not use a home telephone is purchased from Aristotle or from one of several other research companies that provide access to cell respondents. Where meaningful,
SurveyUSA indicates the percentage of respondents who use a home phone and the percentage who do not, and crosstabs by this distinction. Responses are minimally weighted to U.S. Census
or voter file targets for gender, age and race. Target (cell) weighting is used. On questionnaires that ask about political party identification, SurveyUSA may or may not weight to Party ID, depending
on client preference. Where necessary, questions and answer choices are rotated to prevent order bias, recency, and latency effects. On some studies, certain populations are over-sampled, so
that the number of unweighted respondents exceeds the number of weighted respondents. Each individual SurveyUSA release contains the date(s) on which interviews are conducted and a
release date. If interviewing for a particular study is conducted in Spanish, or in any other foreign language, it will be noted on the specific release. If no notation appears, interviews are conducted
in English. Where respondents are filtered, such as adults, filtered to registered voters, in turn filtered to likely voters, SurveyUSA describes the filtering on the specific release. On pre-election polls
in geographies with early voting, SurveyUSA differentiates between respondents who have already voted and those who are likely to vote but have not yet done so. On research completed prior to
12/31/16, SurveyUSA assigned to each question within the instrument a theoretical margin of sampling error. Effective 01/01/17, SurveyUSA assigns to each question within the instrument a
credibility interval, which better reflects the sampling uncertainties associated with gathering some percentage of respondent answers using non-probability sample. Though commonly cited in the
presentation of research results, “sampling error” is only one of many types of error that may influence the outcome of an opinion research study. More practical concerns include the way in which
questions are worded and ordered, the inability to contact some, the refusal of others to be interviewed, and the difficulty of translating each questionnaire into all possible languages and dialects.
Non-sampling errors cannot be quantified. This statement conforms to the principles of disclosure as recommended by the National Council on Public Polls (NCPP). Questions about SurveyUSA
research can be addressed to editor@surveyusa.com.
