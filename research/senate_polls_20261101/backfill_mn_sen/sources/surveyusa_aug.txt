With Inflation Topping List of Voter Issues, DFL Candidates Have Advantage in 3 of 5 Statewide Minnesota Races; Other Two Are Tied:
DFL candidates have the early advantage in the November elections for US Senate and Secretary of State, and are poised to easily retain the governorship, according to SurveyUSA's latest
exclusive polling for KSTP-TV Minneapolis, KAAL-TV Rochester, and WDIO-TV Duluth.
In the contest to replace Tim Walz as Governor , DFL candidate Amy Klobuchar today takes 52% of the vote to Republican Lisa Demuth's 36%, with 1% voting for Green Party candidate Steven
Young and 12% undecided. Demuth leads by 81 points among very conservative and 65 points among somewhat conservative voters; by 74 points among Republicans; and by 73 points among
those who voted for Donald Trump in 2024. Among those voters who say fraud is the most important issue in the election &ndash; 15% of the electorate &ndash; Demuth leads by 69 points.
Among those who say immigration is the top issue &ndash; 8% of likely voters &ndash; Demuth leads by 39. Demuth also leads by 10 points in rural Minnesota, by 9 in Western MN, and by 8 points
among those with high school educations. Klobuchar leads by 99 points among somewhat liberal and by 91 points among very liberal voters, by 94 points among Democrats, and by 90 points
among those who voted for Kamala Harris in 2024. On the issues, Klobuchar leads by 64 points among the 11% who say healthcare is the most important issue, by 35 points among the 35% who
say inflation and the cost of living is most important, by 34 points among the 5% most focused on the war in Iran, and by 16 points among the 6% who say gas prices are the top issue. Klobuchar
leads by 32 points among moderates and by 17 points among independent voters, and by an overwhelming 43 points among the youngest voters, but by smaller margins of 9 points among those
35 to 49, 12 points among those 50 to 64, and 6 points among those 65+
.
The race to replace Tina Smith in the US Senate is tighter, with DFL candidate Peggy Flanagan starting off 5 points ahead of Republican Michele Tafoya, 46% to 41%, with 1% each saying they
would vote for Libertarian Rebecca Whiting and independent Marisa Simonetti, and 11% undecided. 89% of liberals and 87% of Democrats vote for Flanagan, while 85% of conservatives and
87% of Republicans vote for Tafoya. Moderates break for Flanagan by an 8-point margin, 44% to 36%, while independents nominally prefer Tafoya by a single point. Men vote for Tafoya by a
6-point margin, women for Flanagan by 16 points, a 22-point gender gap. Issues break down similarly as in the gubernatorial race, though Flanagan's lead on inflation/cost of living voters is 17
points, half that of Klobuchar's on that issue, and Tafoya has a slim 2-point advantage among those who say gas prices are the top issue, while those same voters prefer Klobuchar by 16 points.
Flanagan leads by 36 points among 18-to-34-year-olds, by 3 among those 35 to 49; Tafoya leads by 5 among voters 50 to 64 and by 8 among seniors. Flanagan is up by 26 points among those
with college degrees; Tafoya leads by 5 points among those who attended some college and by 22 among those with high school educations. Regionally, Flanagan leads by 15 points in the
greater Twin Cities area, by 18 points in urban parts of the state, and by 13 points in the suburbs; Tafoya leads by 22 points in Western MN, by 8 in the Northeast, and by 7 in Southern Minnesota,
as well as by 20 points among rural voters.
Incumbent DFL Attorney General Keith Ellison and Republican challenger Ron Schutz are tied today, 43% each, with 15% of likely voters telling SurveyUSA they are undecided. Schutz has never
led Ellison in three previous polls on the race over the summer, but is up 7 points from the most recent poll, two weeks ago, while Ellison is down 1 point. Schutz leads by 10 points among men,
Ellison by 12 among women &ndash; a 22-point gender gap identical to that in the Senate race. Moderates prefer the DFL candidate by 7 points, but independents prefer the Republican by 10.
Further down the ticket, incumbent DFL candidate Steve Simon leads Republican Tad Jude by 7 points in the Secretary of State election, 41% to 34%. 4% say they will vote for a third-party
candidate; 20% say they are undecided, making any outcome possible in November.
The race for State Auditor , like that for Attorney General, is tied, with the DFL's Zach Filipovich and Republican Scott Jensen each taking 39% of the vote today, 3% voting for another party's
candidate, and 19% undecided.

About the Research / Filtering: SurveyUSA interviewed 900 adults from the state of Minnesota 08/13/26 through 08/17/26 using nonprobability sample of internet panel members provided by
Cint USA. Of the adults, 800 were identified as being registered to vote; of the registered voters, 661 were identified as being probably or definitely likely to vote in the November general election
and asked the substantive questions which follow. The combined pool of survey respondents was weighted to US Census ACS targets for gender, age, race, education, and home ownership. In
an alternate scenario , SurveyUSA also included less-likely voters &ndash; the 12% of registered voters who said they might or might not vote in November &ndash; in the likely voter universe.

Statement of Methodology: SurveyUSA is an independent, non-partisan, apolitical research company that conducts opinion surveys for media, academic institutions, commercial clients,
non-profits, governments, agencies, and elected officials. SurveyUSA opinion research is conducted using a methodology optimized for each particular project. In some cases, this means data is
collected 100% by telephone; in some cases, 100% online; and in other cases, a blend of the two. For those projects that are conducted “mixed-mode” (or “multi-mode”): Respondents who have a
home (landline) telephone are interviewed by phone, sometimes using live interviewers, other times using the recorded voice of a professional announcer. The youngest male is requested on
approximately 30% of calls to home phones, the youngest adult is requested on approximately 70% of calls. This method of intra-household selection reduces the potential for age and gender
imbalance in the unweighted sample. Re-attempts are made to busy signals, no-answers, and answering machines. Landline telephone sample is purchased from Aristotle of Washington DC.
Respondents who do not use a home telephone are interviewed on an electronic device, which means, for some projects, that call-center employees hand-dial cell phones and interview
respondents verbally on the respondent’s cell phone, and means, for other projects, that SurveyUSA displays the questions visually on the respondent’s phone, tablet, or other device. Sample for
respondents who do not use a home telephone is purchased from Aristotle or from one of several other research companies that provide access to cell respondents. Where meaningful,
SurveyUSA indicates the percentage of respondents who use a home phone and the percentage who do not, and crosstabs by this distinction. Responses are minimally weighted to U.S. Census
or voter file targets for gender, age and race. Target (cell) weighting is used. On questionnaires that ask about political party identification, SurveyUSA may or may not weight to Party ID, depending
on client preference. Where necessary, questions and answer choices are rotated to prevent order bias, recency, and latency effects. On some studies, certain populations are over-sampled, so
that the number of unweighted respondents exceeds the number of weighted respondents. Each individual SurveyUSA release contains the date(s) on which interviews are conducted and a
release date. If interviewing for a particular study is conducted in Spanish, or in any other foreign language, it will be noted on the specific release. If no notation appears, interviews are conducted
in English. Where respondents are filtered, such as adults, filtered to registered voters, in turn filtered to likely voters, SurveyUSA describes the filtering on the specific release. On pre-election polls
in geographies with early voting, SurveyUSA differentiates between respondents who have already voted and those who are likely to vote but have not yet done so. On research completed prior to
12/31/16, SurveyUSA assigned to each question within the instrument a theoretical margin of sampling error. Effective 01/01/17, SurveyUSA assigns to each question within the instrument a
credibility interval, which better reflects the sampling uncertainties associated with gathering some percentage of respondent answers using non-probability sample. Though commonly cited in the
presentation of research results, “sampling error” is only one of many types of error that may influence the outcome of an opinion research study. More practical concerns include the way in which
questions are worded and ordered, the inability to contact some, the refusal of others to be interviewed, and the difficulty of translating each questionnaire into all possible languages and dialects.
Non-sampling errors cannot be quantified. This statement conforms to the principles of disclosure as recommended by the National Council on Public Polls (NCPP). Questions about SurveyUSA
research can be addressed to editor@surveyusa.com.
